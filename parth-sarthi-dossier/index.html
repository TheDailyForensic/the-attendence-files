The Record

Ninety-two school days were held between 01-Jun-2026 and 28-Sep-2026. On forty of them Parth Sarthi was marked present. On the remaining fifty-two he was not. The resulting rate of 43.5 per cent places him 1417th of 1420 students in the school and thirtieth of thirty in his own class, 14.1 points below the next-lowest classmate. He is not at the bottom of a tight group. He is separated from the rest of the class. What follows is the register set out in full: the months, the Wednesdays, the blocks, the weekends, and the sixteen consecutive days in the middle of the year on which he did not appear at all. The register does not record where he was; it records, with some emphasis, that he was not.

I · How To Read This Register

Three ideas carry the whole document. The register marks each student on each school day as either Present or Absent, and nothing else is used here; half-day codes are excluded from every calculation. The phrase “N of M” means present on N days out of M possible days of that type, so “7 of 16” for Wednesday means the school held 16 Wednesdays in the period and he was present on 7 of them. The rate is always the same formula, always in percent: rate = (N present / M possible days) × 100. So 7 of 16 = 43.8%.

Two denominator warnings apply throughout. Weekday percentages use only the days that weekday actually occurred, and September is incomplete because the data stops on 28-Sep, so September has fewer Mondays and Fridays than June had. The full-year figure uses all 92 school days, including 8 Saturdays and 1 Sunday. Sunday occurs exactly once in this register and has not been seen since. Our investigative team worked around the clock for nearly twelve consecutive minutes, then read the same CSV a second time, out of an abundance of caution. The second read matched the first, exactly as hoped.

XVII · The Verdict

Parth’s record is not a gradual decline and it is not a scheduling quirk. It is a stable pattern with two states: attending normally, or gone entirely. The pattern has been kept with more consistency than the attendance itself.

Findings
  • He oscillates between weeks at 60 to 80% and weeks at 0%. He has had three separate 0% weeks.
  • The mean gap between absence episodes is 1.7 school days, and he has only a 44.4% chance of returning the day after an absence begins. This is why his absences arrive as blocks, including one block of 16 consecutive days.
  • His absences are spread almost evenly across Monday to Friday, which argues against a single-day dodge pattern. Wednesday is the exception worth noting: 43.8%, falling every single month, 75 down to 25, and on all 9 missed Wednesdays the school was above 90% attendance.
  • 35 of his 52 absences, or 67.3%, fall on days when his class was at 75% attendance or better. School closures do not explain the record. The school showed up; he did not.
  • His best-ever 10-day window is 70%. He has never held an 80% window. The ceiling is lower than the average.
  • He is 1417th of 1420 in the school and last in his class of 30, with 52 absences against a class median of 16.
  • Current trajectory is downward again: 8 of the last 10 sessions absent, and 4 consecutive absences active right now. The streak is live.
